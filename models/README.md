### The openWakeWord feature models: read this before shipping

The openWakeWord README says the **code is Apache-2.0** and that "all of the
included pre-trained models are licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 due to the
inclusion of datasets with unknown or restrictive licensing as part of the
training data". It does not say which side `melspectrogram.onnx` and
`embedding_model.onnx` fall on. What the evidence says:

- `embedding_model.onnx` is described by the README as Google's Apache-2.0
  `speech_embedding` module, manually re-implemented. Nothing in the README ties
  the CC BY-NC-SA reason (restrictive training datasets) to it, but the
  re-implemented weights are published by openWakeWord, not by Google.
- `melspectrogram.onnx` is "an ONNX implementation of Torch's melspectrogram
  function with fixed parameters": no learned weights.
- The maintainer has **not answered** the question. It is open as
  dscripka/openWakeWord issues #313, #338, #346 and #348 (the last one commented
  on 2026-09-28 and 2026-10-09), asking exactly this and proposing a NOTICE file.
  The two SHA-256s quoted there match the files `fetch-models.sh` pins.

Conclusion: **not established as non-commercial, and not confirmed as
Apache-2.0 by the maintainer.** The only explicit non-commercial grant in the
README covers the pre-trained wake-word models (alexa, hey_jarvis, hey_mycroft,
hey_rhasspy, timer, weather), and those are never downloaded or used here. If
this ever ships commercially, get a written answer on #348 first (or swap the
front end for one with a clear licence); until then treat the two feature
models as Apache-2.0-by-provenance and credit Google and openWakeWord in the
about box.

Also not used, on licence grounds: the HF dataset
`davidscripka/openwakeword_features` (ACAV100M-derived features, tagged
`cc-by-nc-sa-4.0`). The head was trained without it, on Kokoro-generated
negatives only, so `telamon.onnx` carries no NonCommercial data.

Build-time only (not shipped): espeak-ng (GPL-3.0) is used by kokoro-onnx to
phonemize text when generating training audio. It is not linked into anything
we distribute. The Rust TTS needs its own phonemizer, see the Kokoro section.

## Wake-word streaming algorithm (exact; reference: `training/wakeword/score_wav.py`)

Written to be ported to Rust with onnxruntime. Per 80 ms chunk of microphone audio:

1. **Input**: 16 kHz mono, raw **int16 sample values cast to f32, not scaled** (range about -32768..32767, no division by 32768).
2. **Chunk**: 1280 samples (80 ms). Process only whole chunks.
3. **Mel call**: take the last **1760** samples = the previous **480** samples + the new 1280. At stream start the 480 are zeros. Feed them as `input [1, 1760]` to `melspectrogram.onnx`. Output `[1, 1, 8, 32]`: **8 frames x 32 mels** per chunk (STFT n_fft 512, hop 160, no padding). **One call per chunk on exactly that 1760-sample window.** Do not batch windows or feed longer audio in one call: the model clamps its output at (maximum of the call - 80 dB), so different call shapes give different values for quiet frames.
4. **Post-transform**: `x = x / 10 + 2` on every mel value (so all-zero input gives -8).
5. **Mel buffer**: append the 8 new frames to a rolling buffer and keep the **last 76 frames**. This is the embedding window: **76 frames, stride 8 frames (one chunk)**.
6. **Embedding**: feed the buffer as `input_1 [1, 76, 32, 1]` to `embedding_model.onnx`; take the 96 floats of `conv2d_19`. That is one embedding per chunk.
7. **Embedding buffer**: keep the **last 16 embeddings**, oldest first.
8. **Head**: feed `[1, 16, 96]` to `telamon.onnx`; `output[0][0]` is the wake score for this chunk.
9. **Detection**: score >= **threshold** (`telamon.json`, 0.90). After a trigger, ignore further triggers for **25 chunks (2 s)**. Typical hit: the score jumps from about 0 to above 0.9 within about 100 ms of the end of "Telamon" and stays high for 4 to 6 chunks.

**Initial state ("warm-up")**: the stream starts as if preceded by silence. Equivalent definition: before the first real chunk, push **40 chunks of zeros** (3.2 s) through steps 3 to 8 and discard their scores. After that, scores are valid from the very first real chunk. Without priming, scores are meaningless until 76 mel frames and 16 embeddings of real audio exist, about **26 chunks (2.1 s)**. (openWakeWord itself starts the mel buffer at ones and ignores the first 5 predictions; we do not reproduce that.) In numbers, the primed state is: mel buffer = 76 copies of the transformed all-zero frame (-8), embedding buffer = 16 copies of the embedding of that buffer; `score_wav.py` computes it by actually running the 40 chunks.

Check your port: `scripts/train-wakeword.sh score tests/audio/hey-telamon-time.wav` prints the per-chunk scores; a Rust port should match them to about 1e-4. `--check` also asserts that the batched pipeline used for training equals the streaming one (max difference 0).

Latency: the wake is flagged about 0.1 s after the word ends (1 to 2 chunks), plus whatever audio buffering the capture adds.

## Silero VAD v5 (for endpointing)

Chunks of **512 samples** (32 ms) at 16 kHz, f32 scaled to **-1..1** (divide int16 by 32768). Feed `input` = **64 samples of context (the last 64 samples of the previous chunk, zeros at the start) + the 512 new = 576 samples**, `state` `[2, 1, 128]` zeros at start and replaced by `stateN` after each call, `sr` = 16000 as an i64 scalar. Output is the speech probability; above 0.5 is speech. Checked on `tests/audio/*.wav`: speech reaches 1.0, trailing silence 0.0. Without the 64-sample context it still runs but gives slightly different values.

## Kokoro (TTS)

Recommendation: **`kokoro-v1.0.onnx` (fp32)** at runtime for now (best quality). `kokoro-v1.0.int8.onnx` is 92 MB instead of 326 MB and about 15% faster on CPU (measured real-time factor 0.22 vs 0.26 with 2 threads), at some quality cost; revisit in the Performant phase. `fetch-models.sh` downloads both.

- `voices-v1.0.bin` is a **NumPy `.npz` archive** (despite the extension) with 54 named arrays (`af_heart`, `bm_george`, ...), each **float32 `[510, 1, 256]`**: one style row per phoneme count.
- **Style row**: with `n` = number of phoneme tokens (not counting the two pads), use row **`n - 1`** (clamped to 509), giving `style [1, 256]`. (kokoro-onnx 0.6.1: `voice[min(n, 510) - 1]`. Earlier kokoro-onnx releases indexed `voice[n]`; the original Python package uses `n - 1`.) The maximum `n` is 510.
- **Tokens**: `[0, id(p1), ..., id(pn), 0]` as i64 `[1, n + 2]`; 0 is the pad/boundary token. `id` comes from the `vocab` object in `kokoro-config.json` (114 symbols, each a single character mapped to an id; `n_token` 178). **Characters not in the vocab are dropped.**
- **Speed**: f32 `[1]`, 0.5 to 2.0 (1.0 is normal). Output `audio` is f32 `[samples]` at **24000 Hz**.
- **Phonemization** (kokoro-onnx 0.6.1, `Tokenizer.phonemize`): `phonemizer.phonemize(text.strip(), lang, preserve_punctuation=True, with_stress=True)` with the espeak-ng backend, `lang` = `en-us` or `en-gb` (voice prefix `a*` is American, `b*` British). Then every character not in the vocab is removed and whitespace is collapsed to single spaces. There are no other replacements: the espeak IPA is used as is (stress marks, the length mark and punctuation `, . ! ? ; :` are all in the vocab; the model uses punctuation for pauses). Examples: "Hey Telamon" gives `hˈeɪ tˈɛləmən`; "tell a man" gives `tˈɛl ɐ mˈæn`. Texts over 510 phonemes are split at sentence, then clause, then word boundaries and synthesized per piece; kokoro-onnx also trims silence from each piece and re-inserts 0.25 s after sentence and 0.1 s after clause punctuation.
- The Rust port therefore needs espeak-ng (GPL-3.0: mind the licence if you link it, or run it as a separate process) or another G2P that produces the same symbol set.

## The "Telamon" head (`telamon.onnx`, 413 KB)

openWakeWord-style head: Flatten, Linear(1536, 64), LayerNorm, ReLU, Dropout, Linear(64, 64), LayerNorm, ReLU, Linear(64, 1), Sigmoid. Input `[1, 16, 96]`, output `[1, 1]`. Exported with `torch.onnx.export` (opset 17). The chosen threshold and a summary of the evaluation are in `telamon.json`.

Training and evaluation are fully synthetic. All code is in `training/wakeword/`, run by `scripts/train-wakeword.sh` (fedora:44 podman container, Python 3.13, CPU torch 2.9.1, kokoro-onnx 0.6.1; roughly 35 minutes end to end on 12 threads, most of it Kokoro generation):

- **Voices**: 28 English Kokoro voices (`af_`, `am_`, `bf_`, `bm_`). **Held out of training entirely**: test `af_nova`, `am_michael`, `bf_emma`, `bm_lewis`; validation (epoch and threshold selection) `af_sky`, `bm_george`. The other 22 are training voices. `tests/audio/*.wav` use the held-out `am_michael`.
- **Positives**: spellings and prefixes ("Telamon", "Hey Telamon", "Hey, Telamon", "Okay Telamon", "Telamon,", "Tellamon", "Telamun", "Telemon", ...) plus hand-written IPA for TEL-uh-mon, speeds 0.8 to 1.2, optionally followed by a spoken command or preceded by chatter.
- **Negatives**: about 1000 templated and hand-written ordinary sentences, 1000 "word salad" lines (random dictionary words, for phonetic coverage), 62 confusables ("telephone", "tell a man", "tell them on", "salmon", "Solomon", "lemon", "Tel Aviv", "telescope", "talisman", "Pokémon", "hey Thomas", "hey Siri", "tell him on Monday", ...) and noise-only clips. Near-homophones of the wake word (Telemann, Teleman, Tolomon, ...) are deliberately excluded from training and evaluation, since no detector can separate them from "Telamon".
- **Augmentation**: random gain, white/pink/brown/hum noise at 0 to 30 dB SNR, exponentially decaying noise as the RIR (T60 0.15 to 0.7 s), random silence padding, always-on dither, plus hard-negative mining during training.
- Not used: openWakeWord's pretrained models and the NonCommercial `openwakeword_features` negative set.

### Evaluation (held-out test voices, threshold 0.90)

- **Recall 0.978** over 960 held-out positive clips (by condition: clean 1.00, SNR 30 dB 0.99, 20 dB 1.00, 10 dB 0.99, 5 dB 0.99, reverb 0.90).
- **False accepts: 2.50 per hour** on ordinary held-out speech plus noise (9 triggers in 3.60 h, 2 s refractory). Most of them are in nonsense word-salad lines with rare words; at least two are ordinary templated sentences.
- Confusable phrases, packed back to back (far denser than real life): 26 of 496 utterances trigger (5%).
- At threshold 0.95: recall 0.954, 1.11 false accepts per hour.

The target was below 0.5 false accepts per hour; **it is not met on this synthetic test**. The numbers are of unknown transfer: everything, positives included, is Kokoro speech, and no real microphone recordings were available. Expect to retune the threshold on real voices; `telamon.json` is the single place to change it.
